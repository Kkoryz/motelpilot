Frequently Asked Questions

Can I hire you just for HR or recruitment without handing over management of the hotel, and what scope options do you offer?

Yes, we provide HR services on their own or as part of full management, and you buy only the modules you need. Full HR planning, executive search, recruitment, payroll, and consulting and policy projects are each available on their own; only ongoing leadership of your property team requires full management.

Who remains the legal employer, and which hiring, pay and dismissal decisions remain with the owner?

Your ownership entity stays the legal employer, and pay scales, yearly wage rises, bonuses and dismissals stay under your final control. Within the budget you approve, we set hourly rates and propose disciplinary measures, but any salary or senior appointment outside that budget needs your approval, and nobody is terminated until you or the manager you nominate signs off.

How do you recruit for my hotel remotely, how long does it take to fill a role, and do I approve each hire?

We recruit, place and onboard candidates remotely and interview them by video, while your named local contact checks original identity and right-to-work documents in person. From the start of the search, hourly roles take 14 to 21 days to fill and management roles take 30 to 45 days. Within the agreed budget, we hire with your general manager, unless you want to approve hires yourself.

How is HR charged — per employee, per hire, per property — and is it included in the management fee?

If we manage your hotel, staff leadership, recruitment and payroll processing are covered by your management fee. For standalone recruitment you pay per hire, and for HR consulting or policy work you pay a fixed project fee. We quote before you sign, based on your headcount, the hires you expect each year, the modules you select and how many properties you have.

Is your HR service worth it for a 20-room motel with five staff, or is it for larger hotels?

There's no minimum headcount, so a 20-room motel with five staff is eligible. For a small property, we concentrate on recruitment, onboarding, payroll and employment compliance, and leave out programmes designed only for larger hotels.

Three properties: challenges, actions and results

32-room motel in Oregon, United States

Revenue management.

Rates stayed the same most weeks, making quiet weekdays hard to fill and leaving busy weekends priced too low.

We reviewed booking pace, historical occupancy and local demand remotely, introduced daily rates by room type and arrival date, adjusted minimum stays around busy weekends, and sent weekly performance summaries and pricing priorities.

Over the first six months compared with the same six months a year earlier, occupancy rose from 58% to 69%, average daily rate increased 9%, and revenue per available room increased 30%.

46-room hotel in Ontario, Canada

OTA management.

Inconsistent room descriptions and unclear cancellation terms on Booking.com and Expedia left the owner spending hours correcting availability and answering avoidable questions.

We corrected listing details, amenities and policies, reorganised owner-supplied photos, aligned room mapping and availability, monitored listing performance, and handled OTA-related guest messages remotely.

In month four compared with the month before takeover, Booking.com listing conversion rose from 2.1% to 2.8% and Expedia listing conversion rose from 1.8% to 2.3%.

The owner's OTA administration fell from eight to two hours per week, comparing the weekly average in month four with the month before takeover.

28-room motel in Queensland, Australia

Full remote management.

The owner handled guest messages, pricing and maintenance coordination without consistent handovers, leading to slow replies and missed follow-ups.

We took over reservations, guest messaging and arrival instructions remotely, coordinated cleaning and maintenance with local contractors carrying out all on-site work, managed pricing and OTA availability through a shared operating calendar, and introduced weekly owner reports covering performance, outstanding issues and approval requests.

In month six compared with the month before takeover, median guest-message response time fell from three hours to 24 minutes, and maintenance requests resolved within 48 hours rose from 54% to 87%.

The owner's routine operational workload fell from 26 to six hours per week, comparing the weekly average in month six with the month before takeover.

Data-Driven Assessment of Your Current Operations

We assess how your property runs today using benchmarking, reporting and technology for upgrading your systems.

  1. Share operating records

    You give us read-only access to 12 months of P&L statements, PMS reports, payroll and timekeeping records, maintenance and work-order logs, and guest reviews and complaint logs, along with your current vendor contracts.

  2. Rebuild missing baselines

    If your labour, maintenance or guest-service records are incomplete, the assessment still goes ahead. We rebuild baselines from payroll exports, vendor invoices and online reviews. We mark the less precise findings as estimates that show direction, not exact figures.

  3. Assessment fee

    You pay a fixed assessment fee. It rises with your room count, your revenue outlets and the number of systems we connect beyond your PMS and channel manager. Rebuilding missing records also raises the fee.

  4. Extra scope

    Analysing more than 3 years of history costs more, and so does reviewing several properties together. If your hotel has more than 200 rooms, you get a custom quote.

  5. Establish complete tracking

    We don't hold back the assessment until your records are perfect. Instead, we set up tracking while we diagnose, which gives you 90 days of complete data for the follow-up review.

  6. Identify recovery priorities

    Our recovery diagnosis examines your financial health, market position, physical condition and operating performance. We trace revenue, cost, staffing and brand-compliance problems to their causes. Then we define corrections and repositioning opportunities specific to your property.
